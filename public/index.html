Listen to the ocean's temperature

NASA satellites measure how warm the sea surface is, every day, all over the planet. Earth Jukebox turns those maps into music. Warmer than normal sounds higher, colder sounds lower. Put on headphones: the ocean plays from your left ear (west) to your right (east). Track A3 plays 145 years of the whole planet, land and ocean, from 1880 to 2025.

What is El Niño?

Every few years, a long strip of the Pacific Ocean along the equator warms up by a degree or more. That warm spell is El Niño. It moves rain and storms around the world: floods in Peru, drought in Australia and Indonesia, and a hotter year for the whole planet.

Scientists watch one patch of that ocean, called Niño 3.4 (the dashed box on the globe, in the middle of the Pacific; it glows while the bell rings). When it is more than half a degree warmer than the rest of the tropics, El Niño is building. In Earth Jukebox, that is when a bell rings.

Reading the colours

The map does not show plain temperature. It shows how far each spot is from normal: its usual temperature for that place and time of year.

ColderNormalWarmer

Blue: colder than normal, a lower note. Cream: about normal. Red: warmer than normal, a higher note.
